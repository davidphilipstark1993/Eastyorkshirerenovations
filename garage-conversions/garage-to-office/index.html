What's different about this conversion

What matters most in a garage-to-office conversion.

Acoustic separation. The main reason people convert a garage into an office rather than using a spare room is separation from the rest of the house. We pay particular attention to sound insulation between the office and any adjoining rooms, which matters more here than for most other conversions given calls and video meetings.

Electrics & connectivity. We plan extra sockets, dedicated circuits where needed for equipment, and network/data cabling back to your router, rather than relying on Wi-Fi alone through a converted structure.

Natural light & glare. Window position is planned around where a desk and screen will sit, to avoid glare on monitors while still bringing in useful daylight.

Mortgage & insurance considerations. If you're planning to run a business with visiting clients or make the office a fully self-contained annexe, it's worth checking with your mortgage lender and home insurer whether this affects your policy — a straightforward home office for your own use typically doesn't, but it's a sensible question to ask before starting work.

Frequently asked questions

Office FAQs.

Will a garage office get decent broadband?

We run network cabling back to your router as part of most office conversions, rather than relying on Wi-Fi alone, particularly for a detached garage further from the house.

Do I need planning permission to use my garage as an office?

Usually not for personal use, though if you plan to run a business with regular visiting clients, it's worth checking with East Riding of Yorkshire Council or Hull City Council, and confirming your mortgage and insurance position.

How much soundproofing does a garage office need?

More than a typical playroom or storage conversion, since calls and video meetings are more sensitive to noise. We assess this for your specific garage and its neighbouring rooms.

East Riding homes

Renovating East Riding homes, from Georgian townhouses to farmhouses.

The East Riding has a much wider mix of houses than the city. Beverley has Georgian and Victorian townhouses around the Minster and Saturday Market, many in conservation areas. Cottingham, Kirk Ella and Swanland have older village centres surrounded by 1930s to 1970s family homes. Further out there are farmhouses and converted barns, and on the coast at Bridlington and Hornsea, houses exposed to wind-driven rain off the North Sea.

That changes the work. Older solid-walled houses need materials that let the walls breathe, and careful replastering to match what’s there. Family homes on bigger plots often have room for a garage conversion, a garden room or an orangery as well as a new kitchen or bathroom. Exposed coastal houses are where we most expect to find penetrating damp rather than rising damp.

Planning in the East Riding

Planning, conservation areas and listed buildings.

Planning and building control across the region are handled by East Riding of Yorkshire Council. Conservation areas and listed buildings are far more common in the East Riding’s market towns and village centres than in newer suburbs, so we check the position for each house rather than assuming the standard permitted development rules apply.

If your house is listed, most alterations inside and out need listed building consent. We’ll flag which parts of the job are likely to need it before work is planned.

Frequently asked questions

Renovating in East Yorkshire: FAQs.

Do you cover the whole of the East Riding?

We cover homes within about 30 miles of Hessle. That takes in the villages west of Hull, Beverley, Cottingham, Brough, Howden, Goole and Driffield, and out to Hornsea and Bridlington. Send us your postcode if you’re not sure.

Is Hull part of East Yorkshire?

Hull is in East Yorkshire, but it has its own council, separate from East Riding of Yorkshire Council. That matters for planning and building control. See renovations in Hull.

Where are you based?

On Station Road in Hessle, near the Humber Bridge. We don’t have other branches; we travel to you.

Do rural and older properties need a different approach?

Often, yes. Older solid-walled houses, farmhouses and listed buildings need materials and repairs that suit how they were built, and more of them are in conservation areas. We check this at the site visit.

Do you do damp surveys across the East Riding?

Yes. A damp survey costs £119, deducted from the cost of any treatment if you accept our quote. Book a damp survey.

Planning a Full House Renovation in Hull & East Yorkshire: What to Expect

What actually happens on a full house renovation, in what order, and what to plan for before work starts.

Get a Quote More guides

A full house renovation involves more trades, in a stricter order, than most people expect. Getting the sequence right is one of the biggest factors in keeping a renovation on schedule, so this guide walks through what typically happens and when.

The typical renovation sequence

  1. Structural work and strip-out: removing anything being replaced, and any structural alterations (e.g. removing a wall), signed off against building regulations where required.
  2. First-fix plumbing, electrics and insulation: pipework and cabling run before walls are closed up, plus any insulation upgrades.
  3. Plastering: skimming walls and ceilings once first-fix work is complete and inspected.
  4. Kitchen and bathroom installation: cabinetry, tiling and fixtures fitted once plaster has cured.
  5. Flooring: fitted once messier trades are finished.
  6. Decorating: the final stage - filling, painting and finishing throughout.
  7. Second-fix and snagging: sockets, switches, door furniture and a final walk-through to catch any outstanding details.

Do you need planning permission or building regulations approval?

Most internal renovation work - new kitchens, bathrooms, re-plastering, rewiring - doesn't need planning permission. Structural changes, such as removing a load-bearing wall, loft conversions and some electrical or plumbing work, do need to meet building regulations, and some may need a building control inspection. Properties in conservation areas (parts of Beverley, for example) or listed buildings may have additional restrictions on materials and alterations.

How to budget and plan

  • Build in a contingency for unexpected issues found once walls, floors or old fittings are opened up - this is common in older East Yorkshire housing stock.
  • Decide on kitchen and bathroom specifications early, since lead times on cabinetry and fixtures can affect the schedule.
  • Agree a payment and project schedule up front so you know what happens and when.
  • If you're staying in the property during the renovation, plan which rooms will be affected and when, particularly for kitchen and bathroom work.

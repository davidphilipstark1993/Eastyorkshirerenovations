What the survey involves

What happens during a damp survey.

We look at the whole picture rather than pointing a meter at one wall. A typical survey covers:

  • Moisture readings across the affected walls and nearby unaffected areas, so we can see how far the damp goes and how it changes with height.
  • The pattern of the damp: where it sits, its shape, any tide marks or salt staining, and whether it follows a pipe, a window or a corner.
  • An outside inspection of gutters, downpipes, pointing, render, window sills, the damp-proof course line and the ground level against the walls.
  • Ventilation and humidity: extractor fans, trickle vents, heating and how the rooms are used, since condensation is one of the most common causes of damp and mould.
  • A talk with you about when the damp appears, whether it’s worse in wet weather or in winter, and any work done on the house before.

Moisture meters are a useful guide but can give misleading readings on their own, for example on walls with salts or foil-backed wallpaper. That’s why we don’t diagnose from readings alone.

Telling damp apart

Rising damp, penetrating damp or condensation?

Rising damp is ground moisture drawn up through the wall where the damp-proof course has failed, been bridged or was never there. It usually sits low on ground-floor walls, often with a tide mark and salt staining, and doesn’t change much with the weather. Read more about rising damp treatment.

Penetrating damp is water getting in from outside through defects in the building: failed pointing, leaking gutters, cracked render or high ground levels. It can appear at any height, is often patchy and usually gets worse after rain. Read more about penetrating damp repairs.

Condensation is moisture from inside the house (cooking, washing, drying clothes, breathing) settling on cold surfaces. It shows up as streaming windows and black mould in corners, behind furniture and on outside walls, and is usually worst in winter. Read more about condensation control.

Some homes have more than one of these at once. The report will say so, and deal with each one separately.

The written report

What’s in your written report.

After the survey you get a written report that covers:

  • Where we found damp and the readings we took
  • What we believe is causing it, and why
  • Photos of the affected areas and any defects outside
  • What we recommend, in order of priority, including simple fixes you can do yourself
  • A quote for any work you’d like us to carry out, including replastering and making good

Frequently asked questions

Damp survey FAQs.

How much does a damp survey cost?

A damp survey costs £119. If you accept our quote for treatment, the £119 is deducted from the cost of the work.

How long does a damp survey take?

It depends on the size of the house and how many areas are affected. We’ll give you an idea of timing when you book.

Will you just try to sell me a damp-proof course?

No. A lot of damp isn’t rising damp at all, and a new damp-proof course won’t fix condensation or a leaking gutter. We recommend what the evidence points to, and if no treatment is needed we’ll say so.

Do I need to do anything before the survey?

Where possible, clear furniture away from the affected walls and let us know about any past damp work, leaks or building work. Don’t redecorate over the damp beforehand, as it hides the evidence we need to see.

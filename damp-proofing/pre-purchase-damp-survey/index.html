Why “damp noted” isn’t enough

Why mortgage surveys often just say “damp noted”.

Mortgage valuations and general home surveys cover the whole property, and the surveyor usually has limited time in each room. If a meter reading is high, the standard response is to note damp and recommend a specialist looks at it. They’re not being unhelpful. It’s simply outside the scope of that survey.

That leaves you with a vague warning and no idea whether it means a quick gutter repair or a major job. Some lenders also ask for a specialist damp report before they’ll go ahead.

What we do

What a pre-purchase damp survey covers.

It’s the same thorough inspection as our standard damp survey: moisture readings, the pattern of the damp, an outside inspection and a check on ventilation. We focus on the areas flagged in your survey and look at the rest of the house for any other signs.

Your report explains what’s causing the damp, whether it’s rising damp, penetrating damp, condensation or a combination, and what it will take to put right. We include a quote for any work, so you have a real figure to take into negotiations rather than a guess.

Frequently asked questions

Pre-purchase damp survey FAQs.

My survey says “damp noted”. Should I pull out of the purchase?

Not necessarily. Many damp problems have simple causes and straightforward fixes. A specialist survey tells you what you’re dealing with, so you can decide with the facts in front of you.

How quickly will I get the report?

We aim to send the written report within [X DAYS] of the survey. Let us know your deadlines when you book.

Can I use your report to negotiate on price?

The report includes our findings and a quote for any work needed, which many buyers share with the seller or agent. How you use it is up to you.

Do I need to be at the survey?

No, though you’re welcome to come. We need access arranged through the seller or estate agent, and we’ll talk you through the findings afterwards.

Building in Beverley

Orangeries for Beverley's townhouses and family homes.

Beverley's centre is built around handsome Georgian and Victorian townhouses close to the Minster and Saturday Market, many within conservation areas where sympathetic materials and finishes matter as much as the end result. Around the edges of town, Molescroft and the newer estates off the Beverley bypass offer more straightforward modern semis and detached family homes.

On older properties, matching the brickwork and roof detailing to the existing building takes more thought, and planning considerations can be tighter, so the size and layout of what's achievable can differ from one street to the next. We'll check this for your specific property as part of the initial consultation.

We have worked across all areas of Beverley, and also cover the villages just outside town, including Cherry Burton and the surrounding countryside. For our wider renovation work in the town, see renovations in Beverley.

Frequently asked questions

Orangery FAQs for Beverley homeowners.

Do I need planning permission for an orangery in Beverley?

It depends on your property and location. Many single-storey rear extensions fall under permitted development, but many of the Georgian and Victorian townhouses in Beverley's centre are within conservation areas, where planning controls are tighter. We'll check this for your address during the consultation.

Can you build an orangery on a Georgian or Victorian townhouse in Beverley?

Often, yes. On period properties we put extra thought into matching the brickwork and roof detailing to the existing building, and we'll assess your plot and any planning constraints on-site before designing around what's realistic.

Do you cover Molescroft and the villages around Beverley?

Yes, we work across Beverley, Molescroft and the villages just outside town, such as Cherry Burton.

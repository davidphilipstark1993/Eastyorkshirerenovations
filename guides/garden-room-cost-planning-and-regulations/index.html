Garden rooms: cost, planning permission and building regulations.

In short: Most garden rooms fall under permitted development as outbuildings and many are exempt from building regulations, though size, height, position and use can change that. How much does a garden room cost?

Size, glazing and whether plumbing is included are the three biggest factors in garden room cost — here's general guidance to help you budget.

General cost ranges

As general UK market guidance: a small, well-insulated garden room with electrics only (a typical spec for a home office) often runs £10,000–£18,000. A larger, mid-spec room with higher-spec glazing and bi-fold doors typically runs £18,000–£30,000. A bespoke design with plumbing for a bar or kitchenette can run £30,000–£50,000 or more.

These are indicative of the wider UK market and aren't East Yorkshire Renovations' confirmed pricing.

What moves the price

Foundation type (concrete base vs. screw piles) depends on ground conditions and can affect cost. Glazing specification and door choice (bi-fold vs. a single door and fixed windows) are also significant, as is whether plumbing is included for a kitchenette, bar or WC.

How much does a garden office cost?

A garden office is usually the simplest, most cost-effective garden room build, since it typically needs electrics only and no plumbing.

General cost range

As general UK market guidance, a small, well-insulated garden office with electrics and data cabling typically runs £10,000–£18,000, rising with size, glazing specification and interior finish. This is indicative of the wider UK market, not East Yorkshire Renovations' confirmed pricing.

What's worth spending on

Good insulation and glazing matter more for a working space than premium interior finishes — a comfortable, well-lit, properly heated office you'll use every day is a better investment than expensive but unnecessary fittings.

Do you need planning permission for a garden room?

Most garden rooms fall under permitted development as an outbuilding, but the details matter — here's the general position.

When permitted development applies

Single-storey outbuildings, including garden rooms, generally fall under permitted development provided they stay within height limits, sit behind the principal elevation of the house, and don't push the total coverage of outbuildings and extensions in the garden over the permitted limit.

When you're more likely to need an application

Using the garden room as separate, self-contained living accommodation — with its own bathroom and kitchen, or as somewhere someone sleeps — is more likely to need planning permission. Listed buildings and conservation areas can also bring tighter restrictions.

Do garden rooms need building regulations approval?

Most straightforward garden rooms under typical size thresholds don't need building regulations approval, but there are exceptions worth knowing about.

The general exemption

Detached single-storey buildings used incidental to the main house (a garden room, office or gym) are often exempt from building regulations provided they're under a set floor area and meet other conditions.

When it changes

Building regulations are more likely to apply if the garden room includes sleeping accommodation, exceeds relevant size thresholds, or sits very close to a boundary, which brings in additional fire safety considerations.

Garden room vs extension: which is right for you?

Both add usable space, but they solve different problems — here's how to think about the choice.

Disruption and timescale

A garden room is built separately from the house, which usually means far less disruption to your daily life during construction compared with an extension that involves knocking through existing walls.

Connection to the house

An extension becomes part of your home's internal layout, which suits a kitchen or bedroom that needs to connect directly to the rest of the house. A garden room works better as genuinely separate space — an office, gym or studio — where separation from the house is actually the point.

Cost

Garden rooms are often cheaper than an equivalent-sized extension, since they avoid the cost of tying into an existing structure, though this varies with specification and plumbing requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Garden rooms: cost, planning permission and building regulations: FAQs.

Is a garden office cheaper than a garden gym?

Often similarly priced for a comparable size and spec, though a gym may need reinforced flooring, and a bar or kitchenette adds plumbing cost neither typically needs.

Do I need planning permission for a garden office?

Often not, if it falls under permitted development as a single-storey outbuilding within height and coverage limits.

Can I use a garden room as a guest bedroom without planning permission?

Occasional guest use is generally treated differently from permanent, self-contained living accommodation, but this is worth checking for your specific plans and property.

Does adding electrics to a garden room trigger building regulations?

Electrical work still needs to comply with Part P requirements and be carried out or certified appropriately, even where the overall structure is otherwise exempt.

Does a garden room add as much value as an extension?

This varies by property and local market, and is worth discussing with a local estate agent for your specific situation rather than assuming either option automatically adds more value.

Awaab's Law and private landlords: what to know about damp and mould.

In short: Awaab's Law already applies to social landlords in England, who must investigate significant damp and mould within 10 working days of being told. The government has committed to extending it to private rented tenancies through the Renters' Rights Act 2025 and is consulting on how, so private landlords should act on damp and mould reports promptly and keep written records now.

By David · Published 7 October 2026

What Awaab's Law requires today

Awaab’s Law came into force for social landlords in England on 27 October 2025. According to GOV.UK, in this first phase landlords must:

  • act on emergency health and safety hazards within 24 hours of them being reported;
  • investigate significant damp and mould within 10 working days of being notified, and make the home safe within five working days after that;
  • give tenants written findings within three working days of an inspection, and consider factors that could put tenants at risk, such as young children, disabilities and health conditions;
  • offer alternative accommodation if the home cannot be made safe in time.

GOV.UK says landlords who do not comply can face court action, enforcement orders, compensation and legal costs.

Where private landlords stand

The government has said Awaab’s Law will be extended to private rented tenancies as part of the Renters’ Rights Act 2025. GOV.UK is currently running a consultation on extending Awaab’s Law to private rented tenancies and to licences, which closes at 11:59pm on 18 December 2026. The consultation page does not set out the timescales or start date for private landlords, so we cannot tell you what they will be.

The rules above are therefore a guide to what private landlords may be asked to do, not a current legal requirement for them. Check GOV.UK for the latest position before you rely on any of it.

What private landlords can do now

Whatever the final rules say, tenants already expect a quick response to damp and mould, and a landlord who can show what was found and what was done is in a much stronger position. Sensible steps are to:

  • log the date every damp or mould report is received and the date you respond;
  • get the cause diagnosed rather than just cleaning the mould, since condensation, penetrating damp and rising damp need different fixes (see damp or condensation?);
  • keep written evidence of the inspection and any repair.

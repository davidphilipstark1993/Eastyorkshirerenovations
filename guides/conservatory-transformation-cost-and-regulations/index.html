Conservatory transformations: cost, planning permission and building regulations.

In short: Most conservatory transformations reuse the existing footprint, so they rarely need planning permission, but replacing the roof can bring the room under building regulations if it loses its conservatory exemption. Cost depends mainly on how much of the structure you replace, and the roof is usually the single most effective upgrade.

How much does a conservatory transformation cost?

Cost depends almost entirely on how much of the existing structure you're replacing, so it's worth thinking in terms of scope rather than a single average figure.

General cost ranges

As general UK market guidance: a roof-only replacement typically runs £5,000–£12,000. A fuller transformation adding flooring, insulation, electrics and decorating typically runs £12,000–£25,000. A complete rebuild including new bi-fold doors, walls and underfloor heating can run £25,000–£40,000 or more.

These are indicative of the wider UK market and aren't East Yorkshire Renovations' confirmed pricing.

What moves the price

Roof system choice (tiled/panel vs. high-performance glazing) is usually the biggest factor, followed by whether doors and windows are being replaced and how much of the interior fit-out (flooring, electrics, plastering) is included.

Do you need planning permission for a conservatory transformation?

Most transformations don't need planning permission, since they reuse the existing footprint — here's when that changes.

When permission usually isn't needed

Because a conservatory transformation typically keeps the existing dwarf walls and footprint, it's usually treated as an internal and roof upgrade rather than a new structure, meaning planning permission generally isn't required.

When it might be

This changes if you're also extending the structure's size or footprint, or if the property is listed. It's worth checking with Hull City Council or East Riding of Yorkshire Council for your specific property if you're unsure.

Do conservatory roof replacements need building regulations approval?

This comes down to the conservatory exemption — whether your finished room still qualifies for it, or whether the transformation removes that status.

The conservatory exemption

Conservatories can be exempt from building regulations if they meet specific criteria, broadly: they're independently heated with separate controls, thermally separated from the main house by external-quality doors, and under a certain floor area.

When a roof replacement changes that

A straightforward roof-only replacement that keeps the room's heating and thermal separation as it was often doesn't affect the exemption. A fuller transformation that removes that separation — for example, opening the room fully into the house or putting it on the main heating system — can mean building regulations now apply to the new roof, glazing and electrics.

Conservatory roof replacement: your options

Replacing the roof is usually the single most effective conservatory upgrade. Here's what the main options actually involve.

Tiled or insulated panel roof

A solid, fully insulated roof finished with tiles or an insulated panel system gives the best thermal performance and the closest experience to a normal room. The trade-off is losing the fully glazed ceiling and the light that comes with it.

High-performance glazed roof

Modern high-performance glazing with solar control and improved insulation keeps more of the original light-filled feel while significantly outperforming an older single-glazed or polycarbonate roof. It's a middle-ground option between keeping full glazing and going fully solid.

Hybrid designs

Some roof systems combine solid sections with glazed panels, giving a partial view of the sky while improving insulation compared with a fully glazed original roof.

What is an insulated conservatory roof?

An insulated conservatory roof replaces a fully glazed or polycarbonate roof with a solid, thermally efficient structure — the single change that does most to fix summer overheating and winter cold.

How it works

Insulated roof systems typically use lightweight insulated panels or a tiled roof structure built over the existing conservatory frame, incorporating proper insulation layers rather than relying on glazing alone. Some systems retain a smaller glazed section or rooflight to keep some natural light.

What it changes

The main benefit is thermal performance — a properly insulated roof dramatically reduces summer heat gain and winter heat loss compared with glass or polycarbonate. It also tends to reduce noise from rain, which is a common complaint with older glazed conservatory roofs.

Frequently asked questions

Conservatory transformations: cost, planning permission and building regulations: FAQs.

Is it cheaper to transform a conservatory or build a new orangery?

Transforming an existing conservatory is generally cheaper than building a new orangery, since you're reusing the existing footprint and dwarf walls rather than starting from foundations.

Does a roof-only replacement ever need planning permission?

Rarely, since it doesn't change the building's footprint, though we'll confirm this for your specific property.

How do I know if my conservatory currently qualifies for the exemption?

We assess this as part of the survey, based on how it's currently heated and separated from the rest of the house.

Will a full rebuild always need building regulations approval?

In most cases, yes, since a full rebuild typically changes the heating and thermal separation arrangement.

Do I lose all the light with a solid roof replacement?

You lose the fully glazed ceiling, but well-designed solid roofs often include rooflights or lantern sections to retain some overhead light.

How long does a roof-only replacement take?

Typically 1-2 weeks for a straightforward replacement, depending on the roof system chosen and the size of the conservatory.

Can any conservatory have its roof upgraded to an insulated system?

Most can, though the existing frame needs to be assessed to confirm it can support the new roof structure. We check this as part of the survey.

Does an insulated roof need planning permission?

Usually not, since it typically doesn't change the conservatory's footprint. Building regulations position depends on the finished specification — see our conservatory transformations page for details.

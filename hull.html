Hull homes

What renovating a house in Hull usually involves.

Most of the city’s older housing is Victorian and Edwardian terraces: the streets off Newland Avenue and Princes Avenue, the bay-fronted villas of the Avenues, and the long terraces off Hessle Road, Anlaby Road, Beverley Road and Holderness Road. These houses tend to need the same things: a kitchen in a narrow back addition, a bathroom squeezed into a box room, lath-and-plaster ceilings that have started to sag, and solid brick walls with no cavity, which makes damp and condensation more likely.

Further out, the 1930s semis around Bricknell and Anlaby Park are usually about opening up: knocking the kitchen and dining room into one, or turning a box room into an ensuite. Post-war estates such as Bransholme and Orchard Park, and newer estates such as Kingswood and Victoria Dock, more often need a tired kitchen or bathroom replaced, or an integral garage turned into a room.

Whichever it is, we work in the right order: damp and structural repairs first, then rewiring and plumbing while the walls are open, then plastering, and only then the new kitchen, bathroom and decorating.

Damp in Hull

Why so many Hull houses have damp, and how we deal with it.

Hull is a low-lying city on the Humber with a high water table, and many homes were flooded in 2007. Add solid walls, suspended timber floors and rooms that have been sealed up with uPVC windows, and you get a lot of rising damp, penetrating damp and condensation, often all being blamed on one another.

So we start with a survey rather than a sales pitch. A damp survey in Hull costs £119, which is deducted if you go ahead with the work. You get a written report of the cause, and a quote that includes the replastering and making good, because we do that ourselves. Not sure what you’re looking at? Read is it damp or condensation?

Planning in Hull

Planning permission and building regulations in Hull.

Planning and building control in the city are handled by Hull City Council. Most internal work, such as a new kitchen or bathroom, doesn’t need planning permission, but some does need building regulations sign-off: removing a wall, new electrical circuits, and changes to drainage or ventilation.

If you live in one of the city’s conservation areas, such as the Avenues, changes to the outside of the house can need permission too. We’ll tell you what applies at the site visit, before you commit to anything.

Frequently asked questions

Renovating in Hull: FAQs.

Do you have an office in Hull?

No. We’re based on Station Road in Hessle, just west of the city boundary, and we come to you for site visits and the work itself.

Which parts of Hull do you cover?

All of it: the city centre and the Avenues, west Hull and Hessle Road, north Hull and Bransholme, east Hull and Holderness Road, Sutton, Orchard Park and Kingswood, plus the villages around the city.

How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Hull?

A typical full bathroom renovation we carry out comes to around £6,000. Room size, the suite and tiles, a wet room and what we find behind the old tiles all change that, so we confirm the price in a written quote. See bathroom renovations.

Do you work on rental properties in Hull?

Yes. We work for landlords and letting agents on refurbishments between tenancies, and on damp and mould. See damp and mould reports for landlords.

Should I sort the damp before renovating?

Yes. New plaster and decorating over an unsolved damp problem will fail. We survey first, treat the cause, then replaster and carry on with the renovation.

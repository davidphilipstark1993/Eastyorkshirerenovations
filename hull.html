Hull property types

Renovating Hull's terraces, villas and suburban homes.

Most of Hull is brick, and a lot of it is old. Victorian and Edwardian terraces fill the streets around Newland Avenue, Princes Avenue and Pearson Park, with grander bay-fronted villas in the Avenues conservation area. Further out you'll find 1930s semis, post-war estates and newer developments such as Kingswood, the Marina and the Fruit Market.

The older terraces are the ones that need most care. Many have solid brick walls with no cavity, and an original damp-proof course that may have failed or been bridged by a raised path or yard. Hull is low-lying and close to the Humber, so wind-driven rain also finds its way through weathered pointing, gutters and exposed gable walls. That is why rising damp, penetrating damp and condensation in rooms with no extraction are all common here, and why a damp patch is worth diagnosing before anyone treats it.

Inside, the work this housing throws up is familiar: lath-and-plaster walls and ceilings that need patching or replacing, small box-room bathrooms, and kitchens that haven't been touched since the 1990s. Access is part of the job too. Narrow hallways and back additions mean we plan strip-out and deliveries around the layout, not around a standard house. Those back-addition kitchens and bathrooms, often with a lean-to or flat roof, are rooms where ventilation and damp are always worth checking before new fittings go in.

If you're not sure whether you have damp, start with a damp survey. It costs £119 including VAT, and the £119 is deducted from the cost of treatment if you go ahead. We carry out the replastering and making good ourselves, so the wall is finished, not just treated.

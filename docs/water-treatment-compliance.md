## 1. Water Fittings Regulations (Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999)
- **Source of truth:** legislation.gov.uk (SI 1999/1148) and GOV.UK guidance
  ([gov.uk/guidance/water-fittings-regulations-specifications-approved-by-the-regulators](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/water-fittings-regulations-specifications-approved-by-the-regulators)).
- **Purpose (PUBLISHED):** to prevent contamination of drinking water supplies, prevent waste,
  and prevent misuse/undue consumption or mis-measurement of water.
- **Backflow prevention (PUBLISHED):** "Every water system shall contain an adequate device or
  devices for preventing backflow of fluid from any appliance, fitting or process." Water
  softener output is classified as **Fluid Category 2** (water with a slight change of
  temperature, taste or odour, not posing a health risk) — this classification requires, per
  the regulations, a single check valve as backflow protection on the softener's inlet.
- **Servicing valves (PUBLISHED):** inlets to water softeners and similar appliances should
  have a servicing valve fitted to allow maintenance.
- **Implication for our business:** every softener/filtration installation must include
  correctly specified backflow protection (appropriate to the Fluid Category of what's being
  installed) and isolation/servicing valves. This is a plumbing-standard requirement, not
  optional best practice — it must be built into what we brief installers to do and what we
  commission/sign off on every job.
- **Note on RO systems:** the Fluid Category classification for reverse osmosis waste/reject
  water and any air-gap/tundish requirements at the drain connection were **not verified in this
  pass** — requires confirmation from WaterRegsUK guidance or a WRAS-listed installation
  instruction for the specific RO product before we install any RO system.

## 2. WRAS approval / WaterRegsUK
- **Naming note:** The domain wras.co.uk now redirects (HTTP 301) to **waterregsuk.co.uk** —
  the consumer/industry-facing guidance site for the Water Regulations Advisory Scheme now
  operates under this name. Use "WaterRegsUK" or "WRAS / Water Regulations Advisory Scheme" as
  the current correct terminology going forward, not an outdated URL.
- **What WRAS approval means (general industry understanding, requires confirmation from
  regulator/product documentation per model):** WRAS approval/listing applies to specific
  **products and materials**, certifying they meet the Water Fittings Regulations' requirements
  (e.g. materials that won't contaminate water, backflow-prevention devices that perform as
  required). It is a **product-level** approval, not a company-level accreditation.
- **Implication for our business:** we cannot claim "WRAS approved" as a blanket business claim.
  At most, once we've selected specific equipment models, we could accurately state that a
  *specific product* carries WRAS approval/listing — and only after checking the WRAS product
  database or the manufacturer's own documentation for that exact model, not assuming brand-wide
  approval.
- **Action before launch:** check each shortlisted product model against the WRAS/WaterRegsUK
  approved products database before making any WRAS-related claim on the website.

## 3. WaterSafe registration
- **What WaterSafe is (PUBLISHED, watersafe.org.uk):** "a free, industry-backed online directory
  that helps you find reliable, registered plumbing professionals" across England, Scotland,
  Wales and Northern Ireland.
- **How a business becomes registered (PUBLISHED):** A plumbing business/sole trader must first
  join one of the **Approved Contractors' Schemes** — **WIAPS, APHC, CIPHE, or SNIPEF** — and
  can then opt in to the free WaterSafe Register listing.
- **Requirements (PUBLISHED):**
  - Minimum **£2 million public liability insurance**, and **£5 million employers' liability**
    cover where applicable.
  - Employ "suitably qualified and competent individuals" — minimum **NVQ Level 2 in
    Mechanical Engineering Services (Plumbing)** or equivalent, plus a **Water Fittings
    Regulations qualification**.
  - At least one qualified plumber per five employees undertaking water-supply work.
  - The business must be **audited within its first 12 months** of membership, then
    periodically thereafter.
  - Approved Contractor Scheme membership fees vary by scheme; one example found (SNIPEF):
    **£160 + VAT annual fee** for members and non-members alike (PUBLISHED, snipef.org) — this
    is one scheme's figure only, not necessarily representative of WIAPS/APHC/CIPHE.
- **What registration allows (PUBLISHED):** registered contractors can carry out certain
  notifiable work without prior notification to the water supplier, and can issue Certificates
  of Compliance.
- **Registration timeline:** not published in the pages reviewed — requires confirmation from
  the scheme once we identify which route (WIAPS/APHC/CIPHE/SNIPEF) is most relevant.
- **Implication for our business/website copy:** We must **not** state "WaterSafe approved,"
  "WRAS approved," "qualified installer" or "certified installer" until we (or our
  subcontracted installer) have actually completed this registration process and hold current,
  valid status. Until then, the accurate wording — already used on the live page — is
  along the lines of *"installation is carried out by an appropriately qualified water-fitting/
  plumbing professional."* This wording should be reviewed once we know exactly who is doing the
  installation (in-house hire vs. subcontracted plumber) and what qualifications/registration
  they personally or their business hold, since the claim must be true of the *actual* installer,
  not aspirational.
- **Source:** [watersafe.org.uk](https://www.watersafe.org.uk/), [watersafe.org.uk/advice/hiring_a_plumber/finding_a_plumber/what_is_a_watersafe_approved_plumbing_business/](https://www.watersafe.org.uk/advice/hiring_a_plumber/finding_a_plumber/what_is_a_watersafe_approved_plumbing_business/), [snipef.org/contractors/certification-schemes/watersafe/](https://snipef.org/contractors/certification-schemes/watersafe/)

## 4. Softened water and sodium — health-related messaging boundaries
- **DWI-attributed guidance (via search summary — re-verify directly against dwi.gov.uk before
  quoting):** water with sodium content up to 200ppm is generally considered safe to drink;
  softened water in most UK hard-water areas is unlikely to exceed this given typical starting
  hardness levels.
- **Common industry recommendation (widely repeated, consistent with DWI's general public
  advice on softened water):** fit an additional unsoftened tap/cold supply for drinking and
  cooking, particularly relevant for infant formula preparation and for people advised to
  monitor sodium intake (e.g. some cardiovascular/hypertension patients).
- **Implication for our business/website copy:** This is precisely why our PREMIUM package
  already includes a dedicated drinking-water tap, and why our FAQ correctly states softened
  water is "safe to drink" while also offering an unsoftened/filtered drinking option. We should
  **not** state a specific sodium ppm figure on the website unless it is sourced directly from
  DWI's own published guidance (not this research summary) and applies generally — the accurate,
  defensible position is the existing FAQ language: encourage an unsoftened/dedicated drinking
  tap as good practice, without quoting a number we haven't verified first-hand.
- **Action before launch:** fetch DWI's own published guidance directly (dwi.gov.uk) to confirm
  the exact figure and wording before adding any specific ppm claim to the website.

## 5. Reverse osmosis — scope limitations
- No UK-specific regulatory limitation on point-of-use (under-sink) RO systems was found beyond
  the general Water Fittings Regulations requirements (correct backflow protection, appropriate
  drain connection). Whole-house RO is technically unusual in UK domestic settings (high reject-
  water ratio, mineral-stripped water reaching the whole house rather than just a drinking tap)
  — this is a practical/product-design consideration rather than a specific regulatory
  prohibition found in this research, but it strongly supports our existing website wording that
  RO is "normally better suited to a dedicated drinking-water application... rather than
  treating the entire house" (keep this wording — it is defensible and matches both regulatory
  practicality and how every researched competitor positions RO).

## 6. Who can legally carry out installation
- UK domestic plumbing/water-fitting work is **not** a single licensed profession in the way
  Gas Safe governs gas work — there is no equivalent single mandatory national licence for
  general plumbing. However:
  - WaterSafe registration (above) is the recognised **voluntary** industry-standard route
    signalling competence and regulatory compliance to consumers.
  - Certain specific work is notifiable to the local water undertaker (e.g. Yorkshire Water)
    under the Water Fittings Regulations, and non-WaterSafe-registered installers/businesses
    must notify the water company directly before certain classes of work, whereas WaterSafe-
    registered installers can self-certify via Compliance Certificates without prior
    notification (PUBLISHED, WaterSafe).
- **Implication for our business:** whether our installer(s) need to be individually WaterSafe-
  registered, or whether we notify Yorkshire Water directly for notifiable work, is a decision
  that depends on our final installer arrangement (in-house vs. subcontracted) and must be
  confirmed with Yorkshire Water and/or WaterSafe directly — **requires confirmation from
  supplier/regulator**, not assumed.

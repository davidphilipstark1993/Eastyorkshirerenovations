Conservatories in Hull

Common conservatory problems on Hull homes.

Many Hull terraces and 1930s semis picked up a rear conservatory extension in the 1990s or 2000s, often with a thin polycarbonate or single-glazed roof that was never really designed for year-round use. On older terraces close to the Humber estuary, damp and cold rear additions are a recurring issue, which makes a proper insulated roof and floor upgrade worth prioritising alongside any cosmetic changes.

Because most transformations keep the existing footprint, planning permission generally isn't needed in Hull — the main thing to check is whether the finished specification (heating and thermal separation from the house) affects the building regulations exemption, which we'll assess for your specific conservatory.

Conservatory roof replacement

Replacing a conservatory roof in Hull.

Most of the problem with an old conservatory is the roof. Polycarbonate and older glass roofs let heat straight through, so the room overheats in summer and loses heat fast in winter. Replacing the roof with an insulated solid roof, usually a lightweight tiled system with a plastered ceiling inside, is the change that makes the biggest difference. Rooflights or a lantern section can keep some light coming in from above.

Before we price a new roof we check the existing frame, walls and base can carry it, because a solid roof is heavier than the one it replaces. Replacing the roof can also bring the room under building regulations if it loses its conservatory exemption. We explain what applies to your conservatory, and our conservatory cost and regulations guide covers it in detail. Inside, we do the plastering, electrics and decorating ourselves.

If the room is too hot in summer or too cold in winter, our guide to making a conservatory usable all year explains which fixes work and which don’t.

Frequently asked questions

Conservatory transformation FAQs for Hull homeowners.

Can you fix a conservatory that's too hot in summer on a Hull terrace?

Yes, this is one of our most common requests. In most cases it comes down to replacing the roof with an insulated tiled or panel system, or a high-performance glazed roof.

Do I need planning permission to transform my conservatory in Hull?

Usually not, since most transformations keep the existing footprint. It's a different situation if you're extending the structure or the property is listed.

Do you work on both older and newer conservatories?

Yes, from 1990s polycarbonate-roofed conservatories on Hull terraces to newer glazed conservatories on more recent developments.

Is a solid roof or a new glass roof better?

A solid, insulated roof does far more for comfort, keeping the room warmer in winter and cooler in summer. A high-performance glass roof keeps more daylight but still runs warmer and colder than a solid one. Rooflights in a solid roof are a common middle ground.

Does a conservatory roof replacement need building regulations approval?

It can. Replacing the roof can take the room out of its conservatory exemption, depending on the new roof and whether the room stays separated from the house. We tell you what applies before work starts.

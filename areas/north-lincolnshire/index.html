Towns we cover

North Lincolnshire towns and what their houses need.

Barton-upon-Humber

The closest town to us, a few minutes over the bridge. Its Georgian and Victorian centre is a conservation area, with solid walls and older plaster that need the right repairs. Damp proofing in Barton.

Brigg

A market town on the River Ancholme with older solid-walled houses in the centre and newer homes on the edges, where condensation is the usual problem. Damp proofing in Brigg.

Scunthorpe

Terraced streets in Crosby and Frodingham built for the steelworks, and large inter-war and post-war estates where kitchens, bathrooms and condensation are the common jobs. Damp proofing in Scunthorpe.

Winterton

A village north of Scunthorpe with older houses in its centre and newer estates around it.

Epworth and Crowle

Towns on the Isle of Axholme, flat, low-lying land that was drained for farming. Older houses here can have damp ground floors, so we check floors and walls carefully before any renovation.

Planning in North Lincolnshire

Planning and building control south of the Humber.

North Lincolnshire is a different council area from East Yorkshire. Planning and building control are handled by North Lincolnshire Council, not East Riding of Yorkshire Council or Hull City Council. Towns such as Barton-upon-Humber and Brigg have conservation areas where changes to the outside of a house can need consent, and listed buildings need listed building consent for most alterations.

We point out anything that is likely to need consent at the site visit, and mention it in a damp survey report, before you commit to the work.
